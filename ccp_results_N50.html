Key Findings

1. The CCP extension is a one-parameter substitution. $T_{\text{MF}}=7 \;\ \to \;\ T^\alpha_{\text{MF}} = F^{-1}(1-\alpha)$ in generate_plans() and mfg_end. The master IP, objective, facility constraints, and routing constraints are completely unchanged from cart_colgen.py.
2. No penalty terms, no second stage, no scenarios. Because $T_{\text{MF}}$ enters $\text{TRT}_p$ additively, the individual chance constraint $P(\text{TRT}_p \le D_p) \ge 1-\alpha$ reduces to an exact deterministic deadline filter. This is not an approximation for this problem structure.
3. Cost increases as service level tightens. At $\sigma_L=0.1$: moving from the deterministic baseline to 90% service level increases cost by ~90% ($3.3M to $6.4M) because a second facility must open to serve patients whose routes are now forced to air+air transport. The mechanism is the same as the $\tau$-tightening effect in the deterministic model — the CCP $\alpha$ parameter is the stochastic analogue of deadline tightness $\tau$.
4. Feasibility boundary depends on ($\sigma_L$, $\alpha$, urgency group). At $\sigma_L=0.2$, $\alpha \le 0.05$: $T^\alpha_{\text{MF}} \ge 9.5$d, making air+air TRT$^\alpha \ge 19.5$d — infeasible for medium-urgency patients (deadline 20d at $\tau=0$). The model returns zero feasible plans and halts cleanly. Recommendation: use $\sigma_L \le 0.2$ with $\alpha \ge 0.10$ as the operational range.
5. $\sigma_L$ and $\alpha$ trade off differently. $\sigma_L$ represents epistemic uncertainty about $T_{\text{MF}}$ distribution spread (calibrated from process data). $\alpha$ is a clinical policy choice (acceptable miss rate). They should be set independently: $\sigma_L$ from historical batch data, $\alpha$ from regulatory or contractual service-level requirements.
