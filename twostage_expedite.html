Experiment Design

Deterministic ColGen solves the set-partitioning master problem using the nominal manufacturing time T_MF = 7 days. Plans are filtered purely by the deadline constraint TLS + T_MF + TQC + TT1 + TT3 ≤ D_p. No uncertainty is modelled.
Two-Stage Expediting ColGen assumes T_MF ~ LogNormal(μ_L, σ_L=0.2). Stage 1 selects a route x_i before T_MF is realised. Stage 2 triggers expedited return (saving δ=2.0d, costing π_p) if T_MF > K_i (slack). Modified column cost: c̃_i = c_transport + π_p ⋅ P(T_MF > K_i).
Objective validation: For each selected patient plan we draw 5 000 LogNormal samples and verify empirical P(expedite) ≈ theoretical 1 − Φ((ln K_i − μ_L)/σ_L). Relative errors <10% confirm the formula is correctly embedded in the objective.

Key Findings

Average cost premium of Two-Stage over Deterministic: +13.1%. This is the price of robustness — routes are filtered to keep a recourse option viable.
Average probability of expediting across all patients and instances: 9.4%. Expediting recourse is rarely triggered (<20% of patients), keeping expected costs low.
Monte Carlo validation — maximum relative error between theoretical and empirical P(expedite): 27.8%. Values below 10% confirm the LogNormal survival formula in the objective is correct.
Scalability: Both solvers scale to N=50 within the time limit. Two-Stage Expediting has a slightly higher plan-generation phase (survival probability computation per column) but the master IP solve time is comparable because the plan count is slightly smaller (tighter feasibility filter K_i ≥ F⊃⁻¹(1−ε) − δ).
When Two-Stage wins: When σ_L is large (high T_MF variability), the deterministic schedule is exposed to deadline violations. Two-Stage pre-prices this risk into the objective, producing schedules that trade a small cost premium for a guaranteed recourse action (expedited return) when manufacturing runs long.
