Key Findings

Two-Stage Expediting vs Deterministic: The best two-stage scenario costs $3,367,017 vs deterministic $3,348,865 — a premium of only 0.5%. On average, 32.2% of patients require expedited return, meaning the active recourse is rarely triggered but available when needed.
CCP vs Deterministic: The best feasible CCP scenario costs $6,389,162 — a premium of 90.8%. CCP pre-emptively inflates TMFE to 8.12d, restricting routes and raising facility costs. This front-loads robustness into the plan-selection stage.
Feasibility: CCP had 1 infeasible scenario(s) (strict deadline filter eliminates too many routes). Two-Stage Expediting had 0 infeasible scenario(s) (relaxed filter via δ). The expediting recourse creates more room for feasible routes.
Modelling philosophy: All three methods use σ_L=0.2 for T_MF uncertainty. Deterministic ignores it. CCP enforces hard probabilistic deadlines (no recourse). Two-Stage Expediting allows the schedule to breathe — routes are selected with recourse in mind, and the cost of uncertainty is explicit in the objective as expected expediting cost.
