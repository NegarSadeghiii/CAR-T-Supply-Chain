Problem Formulation

Common Notation

$P$patients$M$manufacturing facilities
$C$collection centers$H$hospitals
$J$transport modes (air, ground)$T$time periods $\{1,\dots,130\}$
$\text{CIM}_m, \text{CVM}_m$facility costs$U^o_{cmj}, U^r_{mhj}$transport costs
$\text{TT}^o_j, \text{TT}^r_j$transport times$T_\text{LS}, T_\text{MF}, T_\text{QC}$stage durations (1, 7, 7 days)

Side-by-Side Comparison

MIP (Time-Indexed)

Objective:

$$\min \sum_p \text{CTM}_p + \sum_p \text{TTC}_p + (C_\text{mat}+C_\text{qc})|P|$$

Variables:

  • $E_m \in \{0,1\}$ — open facility
  • $Y^o_{pcmjt}, Y^r_{pmhjt} \in \{0,1\}$ — shipments at each time $t$
  • 7 flow variables (OUTC, LSR, LSA, INM, OUTM, MSO, FTD)

Key constraints:

  • 8 flow-balance constraints (MSB1–MSB8)
  • Per-period capacity (CAP1 + CAPCON1)
  • Hard deadline: $\text{TRT}_p \le D_p$
  • At most 2 facilities

Size: $O(|P| \cdot |C| \cdot |M| \cdot |J| \cdot |T|) \approx 86{,}000$ binaries for N=15

Column Generation (Plan-Based)

Objective:

$$\min \sum_i c_i\, x_i + \sum_m f_m\, z_m + (C_\text{mat}+C_\text{qc})|P|$$

Variables:

  • $z_m \in \{0,1\}$ — open facility
  • $x_i \in \{0,1\}$ — select plan $i$ for a patient
  • A plan = (facility $m$, outbound mode $j$, return mode $k$)

Key constraints:

  • $\sum_{i \in \text{Plans}_p} x_i = 1$ — one plan per patient
  • Capacity: entering(t) − leaving(t) $\le$ FCAP − recent entries
  • $x_i \le z_{m(i)}$ — plan uses open facility
  • Hard deadline enforced at plan generation (infeasible plans excluded)

Size: $O(|P| \cdot |M| \cdot |J|^2) \approx 258$ plans for N=15

Why ColGen is equivalent: Both formulations define the same feasible region for integer solutions. ColGen avoids creating 86,000+ variables by pre-enumerating only the ~258 feasible patient plans. The hard deadline is enforced by filtering plans during generation — plans with $\text{TRT} > D_p$ are never created.

Key Findings

1. Column Generation finds exact optimal solutions. Across all tested instances (N=5–50), ColGen achieves 0.00% optimality gap vs the MIP when both solve. It is an exact method, not a heuristic.
2. ColGen is 1,858–5,756x faster. Sub-second solve times (0.1–0.5s) vs minutes for the MIP. Scales to N=50 where the MIP fails at N≥15.
3. The MIP becomes intractable at N≥15. The time-indexed formulation creates 86,000+ binary variables and 374,000+ constraints for just 15 patients. The solver either times out or falsely reports infeasibility. ColGen avoids this by generating only ~258 feasible plans.
4. Facility cost dominates (95%+ of total). Transport cost is 1–3% of total. The critical decision is how many manufacturing facilities to open (1 vs 2), not which transport mode to use. Transport mode matters for feasibility (meeting deadlines) but not for cost.
5. N=20 is the tipping point. Tightening deadlines at N=20 forces a second facility, increasing cost by 87.6%. At other sizes, tightening only switches transport modes (+0.3–1.2% cost). This is a non-linear, binary effect that planners must anticipate.
6. Benders has a structural weakness. At loose deadlines, Benders opens extra facilities because its master problem lacks visibility into transport mode interactions. ColGen avoids this because each plan encodes the complete (facility + transport + timing) decision.
7. Economies of scale. Per-patient cost drops from $624K (N=5) to $143K (N=50) — a 77% reduction. Facility costs are amortized across more patients, making larger patient volumes significantly more cost-effective.
